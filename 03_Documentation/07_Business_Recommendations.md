# Recommendations

## 1. Focus on Increasing Repeat Purchases

The purchase frequency analysis showed that Occasional Buyers (2,454) and One-Time Buyers (1,623) formed the largest groups of customers. The business could study their purchasing patterns and identify ways to encourage repeat purchases. Understanding what prevents these customers from ordering more frequently could help improve customer retention.

## 2. Give More Attention to High-Contributing Customers

The revenue contribution analysis showed that the top 20% of customers contributed 77.26% of total gross customer spending, while the top 10% contributed 63.93%.

The business should regularly monitor purchasing activity among these high-contributing customers and understand changes in their spending patterns. Maintaining good relationships with these customers could help protect an important share of customer spending.

## 3. Consider Both Purchase Quantity and Customer Spending

The purchase volume and spending analyses showed that customers purchasing the largest quantities were not always the highest spenders.

The business should consider both purchase quantity and spending when evaluating customer value instead of relying on order quantities alone.

## 4. Understand Inactive Customer Behavior

RFM segmentation identified Lost Customers as the largest segment, with 1,565 customers. The business could examine their previous purchasing patterns and identify possible reasons for their inactivity.

Based on these findings, suitable re-engagement or retention strategies could be planned.

## 5. Use RFM Segments for Different Customer Strategies

The RFM analysis identified different customer groups, including 1,271 Champions (21.62%), 608 Loyal Customers (10.34%), 556 Potential Loyalists (9.46%), 485 Big Spenders (8.25%), 586 At Risk customers (9.97%), 1,565 Lost Customers (26.62%), and 807 Regular Customers (13.73%).

Champions contributed 67.98% of total gross customer spending. The business could focus on retaining Champions, encouraging Potential Loyalists to purchase more frequently, and understanding the inactivity of Lost and At Risk customers.

Different approaches for each segment could help the business manage customer relationships more effectively.

## Note on Spending Figures

The revenue contribution percentages in these recommendations are based on gross customer spending from retained transactions with positive quantities and positive prices. They should not be interpreted as net revenue after returns and cancellations.
