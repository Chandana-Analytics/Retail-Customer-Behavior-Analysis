# Insights From the Data

## 1. Customer Purchase Frequency Varied Across the Customer Base

The purchase frequency analysis showed that Occasional Buyers formed the largest group with 2,454 customers, followed by 1,623 One-Time Buyers. There were 925 Frequent Buyers and 876 Very Frequent Buyers.

This showed that most customers made purchases occasionally or only once, while a smaller group purchased more frequently.

## 2. A Small Group of Customers Contributed Most of the Spending

The revenue contribution analysis showed that the top 20% of customers contributed 77.26% of total gross customer spending. The top 10% alone contributed 63.93%.

This showed that customer spending was concentrated among a relatively small group of customers.

## 3. Higher Purchase Quantity Did Not Always Mean Higher Customer Value

The purchase volume analysis showed that customers who purchased the largest quantities were not always the highest spenders.

This indicated that purchase quantity alone was not enough to understand customer value, as the prices of products purchased also influenced total spending.

## 4. Customer Activity Varied Across RFM Segments

The RFM analysis identified 1,271 Champions and 1,565 Lost Customers.

Champions represented customers with strong recent purchasing activity, frequency, and spending, while Lost Customers formed the largest segment, with 1,565 customers (26.62%). This showed differences in customer activity, purchase frequency, and spending across the customer base.

The RFM revenue contribution analysis also showed that Champions contributed 67.98% of total gross customer spending.

## 5. Customer Behavior Was Better Understood by Combining Multiple Metrics

Each analysis provided a different view of customer behavior. Purchase frequency explained how often customers ordered, spending measured their monetary contribution, purchase volume showed how many products they purchased, and recency helped understand how recently they made a purchase.

RFM segmentation combined recency, frequency, and monetary values to provide a broader view of customer behavior. Looking at these metrics together helped me understand customer purchasing patterns more clearly than relying on a single measure.

A recency value of 0 means that a customer purchased on the latest transaction date available in the dataset, December 9, 2011. It does not indicate a purchase on the present date.

## Spending Calculation Note

The spending figures in this project represent gross customer spending calculated from retained transactions with positive quantities and positive prices. Cancelled invoice rows and negative-quantity return transactions were excluded. However, some original purchase transactions associated with cancelled invoices may remain if they were not themselves removed. Therefore, these figures should not be interpreted as net revenue after returns and cancellations.
